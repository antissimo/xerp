## 1. What real ERPs agree on (research, 2026-10-08)

Sources are listed in section 5. Statements marked *(unverified)* come from the architect's prior knowledge and
were not confirmed against a page opened in this iteration.

### Stable core — the systems agree

| Topic | Agreement | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Item master | An item has a code, a name, a group/category, a *base (stock) unit of measure*, a flag for stocked vs. service, and can be disabled instead of deleted. Some attributes freeze once transactions exist. | ERPNext Item: "Maintain Stock" creates stock ledger entries; a disabled item "cannot be selected in any transaction"; serial/batch setting cannot change after a stock entry. BC: base unit of measure on the item card "defines how you store it in inventory". |
| Units of measure | A global list of units, plus conversion to the item's base unit. Stock is always kept in the base unit; purchase and sales documents may use another unit and are converted. | BC: per-item "Qty. per Unit of Measure", base unit must be 1, separate default sales/purchase units, quantity rounding precision. ERPNext: per-item UoM conversion table (500 Nos = 1 Kg). Odoo: stock unit vs. purchase unit, automatic conversion. |
| Stock is a ledger | Quantity on hand is derived from immutable movement entries written when a document is posted/submitted; documents, not users, write the ledger. | ERPNext: Stock Ledger Entries are generated by Delivery Note, Purchase Receipt, Stock Entry, Stock Reconciliation (and invoices with "Update Stock"). BC: posting creates Item Ledger Entries per line. Odoo: counts are corrected by a move to an Inventory Loss location, not by editing a quantity. |
| Quantity vs. value | Quantity movement and cost are separate concerns; valuation method (FIFO / moving average / standard) is a setting on top of the quantity ledger. | BC: item ledger entries (quantity) with one or more value entries (cost), periodically reconciled to G/L (search-result summary only). ERPNext: FIFO or Moving Average per item. Odoo: Standard / AVCO / FIFO per category; periodic (manual) valuation is the default, perpetual is opt-in. |
| Sales/purchase flow | quotation -> order -> delivery/receipt -> invoice -> payment. Orders reserve but do not post; delivery/receipt moves stock; invoice creates the receivable/payable; partial delivery and partial invoicing are normal. Later documents are created *from* earlier ones and keep the link. | SAP B1: order only raises committed quantity; delivery reduces stock (and posts COGS under perpetual inventory); A/R invoice debits customer and credits revenue/tax; only the invoice is mandatory. BC: "Qty. to Ship"/"Qty. to Invoice", cannot invoice from an order what has not shipped; a direct sales invoice exists for sales without shipment tracking. Odoo: invoice policy "ordered" vs "delivered" quantities. |
| Posting is final | Posted documents and ledger entries are not edited; mistakes are fixed with a reversing/correcting document (credit memo, return, reversal). | SAP B1: a posted journal entry "cannot be changed, only reversed". BC: fields affecting the audit trail require reverse/undo; unpaid posted invoices are corrected via an automatic credit memo. ERPNext: a submitted entry is changed by cancel + amend. |
| Numbering | Document numbers come from configurable series per document type; financial documents must be gapless and in sequence; master data and drafts may have gaps or manual numbers. | BC: gaps not allowed by default "by law"; "Allow Gaps in Nos." for non-financial series; "Manual Nos."; date-effective series lines; max 20 chars. ERPNext: prefix patterns with `####`, `YYYY`, `FY` tokens, several series per document type. |
| General ledger | Chart of accounts; balanced journal entries; sub-ledgers (customer, vendor, item) reach G/L accounts through configuration (posting groups / category accounts), not hard-coded account numbers. | BC: General Posting Setup maps business x product posting groups to G/L accounts; account categories drive financial statements. Odoo: valuation/input/output accounts on the product category. |
| Access control | Role/permission sets restrict who can edit masters and post. | BC: Business Manager / Accountant roles, permission sets. |

### Where they differ — and what we choose

| Topic | Variants | Our choice |
|---|---|---|
| Stock location model | Odoo: everything is a move between hierarchical locations, with virtual vendor/customer/inventory-loss/transit locations. ERPNext/BC: explicit movement types (receipt, issue, transfer) per warehouse. | Explicit document types per warehouse; bins/locations later (ADR-0007). |
| Customers vs. suppliers | Odoo: one partner record with roles *(unverified)*. BC, ERPNext: separate Customer and Vendor/Supplier masters. SAP B1: one Business Partner master with a type. | One `Partner` with `isCustomer` / `isSupplier` flags — matches the existing scaffold and Odoo/SAP B1; many small companies trade both ways with the same counterparty. |
| Multi-company | BC and SAP B1 separate companies physically *(unverified)*. Odoo/ERPNext: a company column plus rules; Odoo adds `check_company` so records cannot link across companies. | Tenant = company; shared schema + `TenantId` everywhere + tenant-inclusive FKs (ADR-0002). |
| Draft numbering | BC numbers the order from one series and the posted invoice from another. ERPNext names the document at creation *(unverified for drafts)*. | Drafts get no legal number; the gapless number is assigned at posting. |
| Stock/accounting coupling | Perpetual (every stock move posts to G/L) vs. periodic (Odoo default). | Quantity ledger first, then valuation, then G/L; perpetual posting is a late roadmap item. |
| Master data codes | BC: user-chosen or series-assigned "No.". Odoo: database id + optional reference. | Server UUID for references plus a mandatory user/agent-chosen `code`, unique per tenant, case-insensitive; series-assigned codes can be added with number series. |

## 2. Sequencing decision: spec 001 is a vertical slice that brings the foundation

The repository violates its own rules (no layers, no tenant, no tests), so nothing can be built correctly on it.
Two options:

- A pure foundation spec (projects, tenancy plumbing, test harness, no feature). Rejected: tenant filtering,
  the error model and the test harness can only be *proven* by a real entity going through all layers;
  a foundation without a feature is verified by nothing and tends to be designed wrong.
- A vertical slice that carries the foundation. **Chosen.** Spec 001 restructures the solution, adds tenants and
  API keys, the error model and the test infrastructure, and proves them with the smallest real entity:
  **units of measure**. UoM is chosen over articles because it has no dependencies, while an article needs a base
  unit (all systems above agree) — so articles come second and are then built once, correctly.

Consequence: the three other scaffold entities (Article, Partner, Warehouse) and their un-tenanted endpoints are
**removed** in spec 001 and return one by one in specs 002 and 004. Keeping them would leave un-scoped queries
on `main`. The builder's pre-created branch `feat/001-articles` should be replaced by
`feat/001-foundation-uom`; articles are `feat/002-articles`.

## 3. Backlog

Scope (owner decisions, 2026-10-08): backend and MCP server only; jurisdiction-neutral; hosted multi-tenant SaaS
with one tenant = one legal entity.

**Owner guidance, 2026-10-09: the ERP itself and its business logic come first.** After 004 the order is
business functionality — stock, purchasing, sales, valuation, accounting, invoices, payments — and platform
items (audit log, permissions, OpenAPI, row-level security, rate limiting) follow. Unwritten specs were
renumbered; 001–004 kept their numbers. From here on the row number is the spec number.

What stays ahead of posting, and why:
- **Document numbers** — a posted document needs a final, unique, gapless number. Spec 005 builds the counter
  (one fixed pattern per document type); *configurable* series are only needed where numbering is a legal matter,
  so they come immediately before invoices (013).
- **Attribution** — every document records `createdBy`, `updatedBy`, `postedBy` and `postedAt`, and ledger
  entries are append-only (ADR-0007). That is the minimum that makes agent posting accountable without the
  audit log.
- Nothing else. Permissions are not needed first: the owner's default is that every key, agent or human, may
  post. The audit log is not needed first: the ledgers are the audit trail of everything that has an effect.

### MVP boundary (owner decision, 2026-10-09)

**The MVP is specs 001–010**: tenants and API keys, units, articles, partners, warehouses, the MCP server,
the stock ledger with receipts, issues, transfers, reversal, unit conversions and stock count, purchase orders
with goods receipt, sales orders with delivery. Rows 11–20 and the "Later" row are post-MVP. No spec beyond
010 is written unless the owner asks.

Open points left by the reviews are in `docs/reviews/open-items.md`; how to run and use the MVP is in
`docs/getting-started.md`.

**What the MVP does.** For each tenant (one company), over HTTP and over MCP with the same rules:
- keeps the masters: units of measure, articles (stock or service) with further units and conversion
  factors, partners (customer and/or supplier), warehouses;
- moves stock only by posted documents — receipt, issue, transfer, count — into an append-only ledger, in the
  article's base unit; stock never goes below zero; a posted document is corrected only by reversing it;
- answers stock on hand per article and warehouse, with what is incoming from purchase orders and what is
  reserved by sales orders;
- keeps purchase and sales orders (draft -> confirmed <-> closed) with prices and amounts, received and
  delivered against in parts by linked receipts and issues, never above the ordered quantity;
- numbers every posted document and confirmed order per tenant without gaps, and records which API key —
  human or agent — created, changed, posted or confirmed it.

**What the MVP does not do.**
- **No invoices.** No sales or purchase invoice, no tax, no credit note; an order is delivered or received
  but never billed (014).
- **No accounting.** No chart of accounts, no journal entries, no value of stock or cost of goods sold, no
  payments or partner balances (011, 012, 015).
- **No permissions.** Every API key of a tenant, human or agent, can do everything in that tenant, including
  posting and creating keys (017).
- **No audit log.** Documents and orders say who created, posted and confirmed them and the ledger is
  append-only, but changes to master data are not recorded beyond `updatedBy` / `updatedAt` (016).

Also absent: row-level security in the database (019), rate limiting (020), an OpenAPI document (018), a web
UI and a CLI (separate projects). The table gives the same limits next to what the MVP has in their place,
with the spec that would add each:

| The MVP has | It lacks | Until |
|---|---|---|
| Unit prices and order amounts on purchase and sales orders | Any value of stock, cost of a receipt, cost of goods sold | 011 |
| Posted receipts and deliveries linked to orders | Invoices, tax, "invoiced quantity" on order lines; an order is fulfilled but never billed | 014 |
| `stock` articles on orders | `service` articles on orders | 014 |
| One unnamed currency, amounts with 2 decimals | A currency setting; other precisions | Later (multi-currency) |
| Fixed number patterns (`SR-`, `SI-`, `ST-`, `SC-`, `PO-`, `SO-`) | Configurable series | 013 |
| Reversal of a whole receipt or delivery | Partial returns; changing a confirmed order (close + new order instead); over-receipt | Later |
| `createdBy` / `postedBy` / `confirmedBy` and append-only ledgers | Audit log of master-data changes; permissions per key (every key may do everything in its tenant) | 016, 017 |

None of these blocks using the MVP for what it does, and none is added to specs 009 or 010. Two limits are
worth stating to anyone who is shown the MVP:
- **It is not ready for production tenants.** Section 4 requires the audit log (016), permissions (017),
  row-level security (019) and rate limiting (020) first; an external client also needs the OpenAPI document
  (018).
- **It has no money side.** Prices on orders are recorded and totalled, nothing more: no valuation, no
  invoices, no ledger accounts, no payments (011, 012, 014, 015).

## 4. Rules for the backlog

- A spec must leave `dotnet build` and `dotnet test` green and `main` free of un-tenanted queries.
- From item 3 on, every operation ships with its HTTP endpoint and its MCP tool in the same spec.
- From item 3 on, acceptance criteria are black-box (HTTP and MCP only) so that the tester can write the tests
  before the builder implements; what is only checkable from inside is marked *(builder)* (architecture §9).
- **Weight of a spec (owner guidance, 2026-10-09):** business rules, document flows and invariants. Transport
  and protocol behaviour — authentication, strict bodies and query strings, the error document, the list
  envelope, MCP result mapping and tool metadata — is established by specs 001–003 and is *inherited*: a new
  spec states that it applies and carries one smoke criterion per kind, not a re-test of every case per route.
- The HTTP API is consumed by separately developed clients (CLI, web UI): no incompatible change within `/api/v1`.
- The core stays jurisdiction-neutral: no country-specific tax, identifier or fiscalisation rules.
- Every posting operation assigns a final document number and records who posted and when; ledger rows are
  append-only. Before production tenants: audit log (016), permissions (017), row-level security (019) and
  rate limiting (020).
